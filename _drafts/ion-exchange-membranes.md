I was looking through the chlor-alkali process today (The electrolysis of salt to make chlorine gas, hydrogen gas, and $$\ce{NaOH}$$). Because at the anode $$\ce{NaOH}$$ is formed, which reacts with $$\ce{Cl2}$$, there needs to be a way to separate the solutions so that no $$\ce{NaOH}$$ gets to the cathode. This is where ion exchange membranes come into play! We need a way to selectively let $$\ce{Na+}$$ into the cathode while keeping the $$\ce{Cl-}$$ out. The membrane used in this process is called Nafion.
