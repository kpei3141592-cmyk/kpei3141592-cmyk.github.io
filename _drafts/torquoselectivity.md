By the Woodward-Hoffmann rules, a $$4\pi$$ ring opening is conrotatory. However, for some asymmetrically substituted cyclobutenes, there is another type of selectivity that depends on which way the ring opens, called torquoselectivity. I originally thought this just progressed through sterics, i.e., the bulky group would rotate outwards, but it turns out that in 1984, the Houk group found some $$4\pi$$ openings that do not do this. For example:

![The ring opening of 3-formylcyclobutene](/assets/img/3-formylcyclobutene-ring-opening.png){: .img-fluid}

The left-hand product (Z) is favored despite the (E) product being thermodynamically favorable.

So what favors inward rotation?

![Molecular orbitals of a $$4\pi$$ ring opening](/assets/img/Torquo-TS-orbitals.png){: .img-fluid}

As we can see, the HOMO has $$\sigma$$ character, and the LUMO has $$\sigma^*$$ character and can interact with substituents' p-orbitals.

![Frontier orbitals of a $$4\pi$$ ring opening along with positions of substituents in either case](/assets/img/frontier-orbital-interaction-torquo.png){: .img-fluid}
